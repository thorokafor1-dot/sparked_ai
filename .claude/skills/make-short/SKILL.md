# Make shorts from a long-form video

Details live in `long-form-to-shorts-video-editing/monkey-app-video-chat/claude.md`. Read that, not the scripts.

## Steps
1. **Get the source:** `python download_source.py <drive link>` into `input/`. Delete it after rendering, because sources are temporary.
2. **Default: let Claude pick the moments.** `python run_pipeline.py --video input/X.mp4 --auto 6` transcribes the whole call, scores every window (`find_moments.py`, borrowed from OpenShorts), and renders the global top 6 as `short_N_v1.mp4`, each starting on its hook and ending on its payoff. The ranked picks with reasons are saved to `work/<stem>_moments.json`. Show the user the scores and reasons with the renders.
3. **Manual override:** when the user names specific moments, pass `--segment a:b --segment c:d ...` instead (the keyword/face hook scan picks the start inside each segment).
4. **Gates run automatically on the new files in `output/`:** specs, black/frozen/silent stretches, loudness, face on screen, and the caption safe zone. Fix whatever fails, then re-render that segment only.
5. **Prioritise flirt moments:** keep the flirt beats, and cut or compress small talk (user feedback).
6. **Open each final render automatically** for the user (user preference).
7. **Hand off to `post-short`** once the user approves.

## Gotchas (keep this list updated)
- **Found 2026-09-27 (first `--auto` run):** Claude's picks were strong (90 and 82), but the reframe lost the face on footage where she sits half out of her own frame or the camera shows only her chin: 0.5-7s gaps flagged by `short-face-on-screen`. That's a `reframe.py` limitation, not a selection bug. Next step: pre-screen candidate moments for face coverage before rendering, and fall back to the next-ranked moment.
- **`--auto` uses its own `<stem>_full_transcript.json`,** because a manual run's transcript only covers the requested segments.
- **The auto-picked hook** isn't always on the right person. The face-on-screen gate catches crop loss, but not a wrong-person opening, so check the first 3s on anything new.
- **Re-rendering one version:** every render writes `output/<name>.render.json` (video, start, duration, transcript). Re-render with `python render_short.py --video .. --start .. --duration .. --transcript .. --out output/<name>_v(N+1).mp4`, with no hook re-analysis needed.
- **Version outputs as `short_N_vK.mp4`.** Only the newest version gets checked.
- **Fixed 2026-09-26:** captions moved to `SOLO_CAPTION_Y=1500` (safe zone), and renders now get a loudnorm pass to -14 LUFS. `short_1_v32` was the first render to pass every gate.
- **Fixed 2026-09-28 (jumpy cuts):** v31 had 16 hard cuts in 30s, most of them re-centring cuts between shots of the same person. `render_short._simplify_plan` now merges adjacent same-person chunks into one shot that pans smoothly (`_solo_pan_crop`), cuts only where the tracked person really switches (`CUT_JUMP`), absorbs sub-0.7s blips, and snaps meme edges to the real frame. v31 -> v36: 16 -> 7 cuts. New gate `short-jumpy-cuts` (`verify_cuts.py`) fails on flash shots under 0.5s and bursts of cuts; cuts inside a meme are ignored via the `cutaways` spans in `<name>.render.json`.
- **Meme cutaways fill the frame** with a blurred, dimmed copy of themselves behind the full clip (no thin letterbox strip, never cropped).
- **Detection is cached** in `work/<stem>_<start>_<dur>_samples.json` (auto-invalidated when the video or `reframe.py` changes), so re-rendering a version takes seconds instead of ~90s.
- **Face gate ignores occlusion:** a gap with no cut inside it where the face comes back within 30% of the frame width of where it left (hand over mouth, motion blur) is not a lost crop.
- Split halves are landscape (1080x960), so `_face_crop` takes a window of the half's own aspect placed on the face's y (`_face_y_finder`, YuNet on 5 frames per chunk). The old full-height sliver centre-cropped away her eyes when she leans in. Solo crops trim `SOLO_TRIM_BOTTOM` source px to hide the app's yellow "Friend" button.
- The face gate has a loose second pass (score 0.4, face >= 25% of frame width) so a hand-over-mouth laugh close-up does not read as "no subject". QA cache keys now include `verify_*.py` and `reframe.py`, so gate-helper fixes re-run cached verdicts.
- When the app auto-enlarges whoever is talking to near-full-screen, the other person shrinks to an unusable sliver and gets misclassified as the SAME "main"/"host" state throughout (both people's enlarged x can land under CUT_JUMP apart), so it renders as a whiplash of solo cuts, not a state change. `_expand_flip_flops` (render_short.py) detects the internal jump from the raw (pre-smoothing) samples, and -- only next to a genuine two-person "split" chunk -- converts it into a real stack. The inactive half MUST be a frozen still (a real photo of that person, held), never a live crop: at that instant the source frame is 100% the other person's background, so any other crop x just shows a fragment of THEIR OWN room, not the missing person. Identity (who's who during the enlarged phase) is resolved by colour-histogram matching the current frame against reference crops from the neighbouring real split (background alone -- her headboard vs his poster wall -- separates them far more reliably than x-position does); ambiguous matches are left as a plain solo cut rather than risk showing the wrong person twice.
- `smooth_states` majority-voting can erase a real, brief (under ~1s) two-person "split" moment sandwiched between two solo shots, relabelling it as the surrounding solo state -- so the plan shows two flat solo chunks with nothing recognisable between them even though the source genuinely cut through a real split. `_recover_hidden_splits` (render_short.py) re-derives these from the raw (non-full_screen) detections and carves them back out before `_expand_flip_flops` runs. It must coalesce consecutive same-state solo chunks first -- otherwise the window-padding search is blind to a real split sample sitting just past an earlier arbitrary chunk boundary, leaving a several-tenths-of-a-second gap with no detection on either side (rendered as a visible blank-background flash). Both this and `_expand_flip_flops` take `opening_end` and must never touch anything before it -- the opening hook is a deliberate forced-"main" override, not a bug to fix.
- A frozen half held longer than ~1.2s reads as broken, not composed. `_build_filter_complex`'s forced-split branch adds a slow zoompan push-in (1.0x -> 1.06x over the chunk's duration) to any freeze past that length, so it looks like a deliberate held reaction shot instead of a stuck frame. Escape the comma inside zoompan's own `min(a,b)` expression (`\,`) -- it's inside one filter's options, not a new filter separator, but ffmpeg's filtergraph parser doesn't know that.
- zoompan gotcha (cost real time -- self-caught twice before this was right, don't repeat): `d` in `zoompan=d=...` is NOT "advance the zoom once per input frame" -- it's "how many output frames to generate from the ONE input frame currently held". Set it to `d=1` and consecutive output frames are pixel-for-pixel IDENTICAL for the whole clip (zoom never advances) even though the filter runs with no errors -- looks fine in a coarse eyeball check, fails a real frame-diff. Set `d=<total output frame count>` instead. That in turn makes the looped image input's stream effectively unbounded (zoompan's `d` does not stop the underlying `-loop 1` source), so concat never reaches EOF on that segment without an explicit `trim=duration=<chunk duration>` placed AFTER the zoompan filter -- without it ffmpeg runs for minutes rendering a 30s clip and never finishes. Self-check for any future freeze-prone shot: `python verify_freeze.py <output.mp4>` (also wired as the `short-frozen-half` QA gate) -- compares real per-frame pixel diffs, not a by-eye read of a couple of sampled stills, which is exactly what missed this the first two times.
- Removed the frozen-photo "stack" idea entirely (see render_short.py history) -- flagged three times running at every duration tried (0.9s/1.7s/2.7s). A held still, however cleanly animated, reads as "a photo" not the person. `_expand_flip_flops` now only does clean solo-to-solo cuts (splitting an internal full-screen identity swap, recovering a real hidden two-person split) -- never fabricates simultaneous visibility from footage that only ever shows one person at a time.
- zoompan needs `d=<total output frame count>`, not `d=1` -- `d=1` leaves consecutive output frames pixel-identical (verified with a direct frame diff, not by eye). With `d=<total frames>` the source loop becomes unbounded, so an explicit `trim=duration=<chunk duration>` after the zoompan filter is required or the render never terminates. (Kept for reference even though the frozen-stack that used this is gone -- the same trap applies anywhere else a still image gets animated.)
- `PositionTrack._raw` is built from `smooth_positions`'s OUTPUT, not the original detections -- it bleeds a real value forward up to its ~1.4s averaging window into genuinely-None gaps, and can drift an otherwise-stable position up to 300px toward whatever a DIFFERENT person's next real detection happens to be (two different enlarged faces can be that close in x). For "is there real adjacent content right now" or "where is this person actually right now" checks, query the RAW (pre-smoothing) samples directly (`_raw_attr_lookup` in render_short.py), not a PositionTrack. Bound a chunk's OWN lookup to `[c_start, c_end)` (exclude the endpoint) -- otherwise its last keypoint can grab the very next chunk's person just because that raw sample lands a fraction of a second closer.
- `_pan_expr`'s snap-vs-interpolate decision must use the RAW (pre-clamp) position jump, not the post-clamp crop-x delta -- clamping (a crop pinned against MAIN_MAX_X_FRAC/HOST_MIN_X_FRAC near a real transition) can shrink a genuine >CUT_JUMP person-swap down to a smaller apparent delta, letting it interpolate into a sweep instead of cutting.
- The opening hook (`opening_end`, forced "main" regardless of real content) can still play through a genuine mid-hook transition the source itself makes (even a full person swap, still collapsed into the same "main"/"host" bucket). Keep per-keypoint DYNAMIC tracking through it regardless -- a tried "static single crop for the whole hook" lost her face for 0.4-0.5s once the real content changed under a stale reference position. The resulting extra cut(s) are real and expected; mark them as a known ignorable seam in the render.json sidecar (`_hook_seam_span`, same "cutaways" mechanism a meme's own internal edit already uses) rather than fighting the crop math to hide them -- shortening the hook's own boundary to dodge it doesn't work either, `_simplify_plan`'s merge pass folds the shortened chunk back into its neighbour.
- Self-check script `verify_freeze.py` (+ `short-frozen-half` QA gate) catches an asymmetric stacked-half freeze via real per-frame pixel diffs -- added after two rounds of "looks fine" by eye turned out not to be.
- A second instance of the "other track nearby" clamp (see the earlier centering-bug entry) was still live: the FRESHNESS check that decides whether to clamp toward MAIN_MAX_X_FRAC/HOST_MIN_X_FRAC (`_solo_pan_crop`'s per-keypoint `fs` override) searched the raw samples with no chunk bounds, so the tail of a real split that had already ENDED still read as "adjacent content right now" for up to 0.75s into the next shot -- clamping it at the start, then releasing a moment later once that staleness expired. Looked exactly like the camera panning in to center itself, because that's what it was: a stable, non-moving shot with a flickering clamp. Fixed by bounding that check to the chunk's own `[c_start, c_end)` too, same as the position-lookup fix.
- Fixed the hook-seam flash for real instead of just ignoring it in QA (user pushed back: "make sure transitions are smooth", not "hide it from the checker"). Two bugs stacked: (1) `_build_crop_plan`/`_simplify_plan`'s merge pass undoes any attempt to shorten the forced-hook boundary, so the fix has to run as a LAST pass directly on the finalised plan (`_realign_hook_boundary`) -- finds where the source itself already cut to different content before the fixed boundary and moves the chunk split there, so the crop and the actual playing pixels agree again instead of showing a blank/wrong-content frame for the last fraction of a second. (2) The snap-point refiner (`_refine_pan_snap`, frame-by-frame biggest-diff scan) was seeding its scan window from an UNALIGNED start time (t0 itself, not snapped to the true 30fps grid) -- every sampled seek landed a fraction of a frame off the real frames, so the scan could step clean over the one frame with the real cut (missed a 58-point diff spike entirely) and pick a much smaller nearby wobble instead, still showing a sliver of source UI chrome. Fixed by snapping seek times to `k/30` and padding one frame on each side of the window (a cut landing exactly at the window edge needs the frame just outside it to compare against, or it's never compared to anything).
- New: every short gets a branded vertical endscreen appended automatically (`_append_endscreen` in render_short.py, asset built by `make_endscreen.py`, reusing long-form's `brand_graphics.py` fonts/logo/gradient helpers for the same on-brand look -- only the 9:16 composition is new, since the long-form endscreen is a hand-made 16:9 PNG that crops badly at 9:16). `ENDSCREEN_SECS = 1.3` -- matched to a user-supplied reference clip's own endscreen duration (~1.2s) and general short-form practice (roughly 1-2s); shorts have no YouTube interactive end-screen elements (unlike long-form's real 5s-minimum requirement), so there's no reason to hold it longer than it takes to register before the viewer swipes. The face-on-screen QA gate exempts a no-face span only when it actually reaches the end of the video and fits the endscreen's own duration (see `short_face_on_screen` in qa/checks_video.py) -- a genuine mid-video face loss that happens to run long is still caught.
- Found a THIRD instance of the "one frame not adjusted yet at a cut" pattern, this time at a real-split-ends-into-solo handoff (_recover_hidden_splits's window padding): a fixed +/-0.1s pad around the last/first confirmed raw sample is really just guessing the midpoint of a 0.2s sampling gap, and the true content transition isn't always there -- it landed a blank/background frame right after the cut. Fixed the SAME way as the hook seam: frame-accurate refinement (_refine_pan_snap) of just that specific gap, not a blanket pass.
- Tried a general-purpose "refine every boundary in the plan" pass first (_refine_all_boundaries, since the user asked to prevent this recurring bug systemically) and it made things WORSE, confirmed directly by the user ("it makes the cutting less smooth") and by cut count jumping 10->17: applying a cut-detector's confidence test to a narrow, arbitrary per-boundary search window is too easily satisfied by ordinary talking-head motion (a head turn, a gesture) that was never a real scene cut, so it invented new cuts at boundaries that were already smooth. Reverted. The lesson: frame-accurate refinement is only safe where there's an independent, already-confirmed reason a real transition exists nearby (a genuine state change the raw detections already establish, like a split starting or ending) -- never as a blind pass over every boundary just because a boundary exists there.
- Found and fixed a THIRD source of the same "one frame not adjusted yet" bug: the internal full-screen identity-swap split (_expand_flip_flops step 1, FLIP_JUMP-based) used to place the boundary at the LATER raw sample's own timestamp (not even a midpoint) -- up to 0.2s after the true cut. Fixed the same way as the other two: frame-accurate refinement (_refine_pan_snap) across just that sample gap, safe because a real identity swap is already proven to exist there (the FLIP_JUMP test), never a blind scan.
- General principle for this whole bug class (write this down, it WILL come up again): a plan boundary built from sparse 0.2s raw-sample timing is a good ESTIMATE of where a real transition happens, never frame-accurate. Refining it to the true frame is always safe PROVIDED something independent already proves a real transition exists nearby (a split starting/ending, a FLIP_JUMP-sized identity swap, a cutaway edge). It is NEVER safe to run that same refinement as a blind pass over every boundary "just in case" -- confirmed directly, that invented new cuts at boundaries that were already smooth (ordinary talking-head motion satisfies a generic cut-confidence test far more often than expected) and made the edit read as LESS smooth, the opposite of the goal.
- Added `verify_lone_frames.py` + the `short-lone-frame` QA gate: scans the whole rendered output for a frame with a large jump on BOTH sides (the shared signature of all three confirmed bugs -- a real cut only ever makes ONE jump, into a now-stable shot). DIFF_THRESH=30 specifically -- every confirmed bug scored 41-83, ordinary talking-head motion (head bob, hand gesture) scored 12-23 on both sides and floods the gate with false positives below that. Skips anything inside a cutaway span from the sidecar (a meme's own fast motion, e.g. someone clapping, produces the same shape without being this bug). This is now a standing, permanent gate -- run on every future short automatically, not just this one.
