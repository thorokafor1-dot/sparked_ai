# Script: "How I Cured My Approach Anxiety"

Written from `video-ideation/ideation_approach_anxiety_cure.md` (Hook Plan, Pacing/Retention Models, Combined Arc) and `long-form-hook-research/infield/hook_patterns.md`. Hook is built on an adapted **Type A** (zero-setup cold open, no branding; adapted because it opens on the pre-approach hesitation, not mid-conversation like the pure pattern) combined with **Type E** (vulnerability/self-deprecating stakes-setting), plus the call-out beat from Model 1 (Casey Zander) in the ideation doc's Pacing/Retention Models. Per that doc's cross-cutting observations, no subscribe ask sits inside the hook window, it's placed after the first payoff (the excuse-list/relatability beat), matching this niche's long-form pattern.

Runtime target: ~7 min (this doc's structure pick, Casey Zander, runs ~8 min; Orion Taraban's message pick runs ~8 min). Talking head to camera (new shoot) intercut with real infield B-roll.

## Footage & Shot Notes
- Two infield clips needed from `long-form-video-editing/talking-head-infield/input/infield/`, neither selected yet (open item in the concept doc):
  1. A visible hesitation moment before an approach, used in the cold open (HOOK), as B-roll under the EXCUSE LIST, and as a 1-2s "before" flash at the start of INFIELD PROOF.
  2. A clip showing a good reaction, used in INFIELD PROOF and INFIELD PROOF 2.
- No dialogue is written for any woman in this script. Every REACTION/OUTCOME line below describes body language and outcome only, the actual audio is whatever's really in the clip.
- One beat (VULNERABILITY) needs the creator's real approach-anxiety story and is marked PLACEHOLDER below. Do not fill it with invented details, numbers, or a story that isn't his own.

## [0:00-0:07] HOOK
ON-SCREEN: no logo, no title card, straight into footage.
FOOTAGE: real infield clip (not yet selected) showing a visible hesitation moment before an approach, mic audio only, no music.
VO: "This is the part nobody puts in the video. Not the approach. The five seconds right before it."

## [0:07-0:12] THESIS
ON-SCREEN: hard cut to talking head, straight to camera.
VO: "That feeling is approach anxiety. And there is exactly one thing that cures it."

## [0:12-0:25] THE CURE, STATED
VO: "Not a script. Not a line. Not a video that hypes you up for ten minutes and then leaves you standing there. The cure is that you go anyway, on purpose, before you feel ready."
ON-SCREEN: talking head continues, no cutaway.

## [0:25-0:40] VULNERABILITY (PLACEHOLDER)
VO: "PLACEHOLDER (say this in your own words, do not invent numbers): the specific moment your approach anxiety was worst, and roughly how many approaches in before it started to ease."
ON-SCREEN: talking head, no on-screen text, let the moment breathe.

## [0:40-0:52] CALL-OUT
VO: "If you've ever made up an excuse in the two seconds before you could've said something, walked too fast, told yourself she's probably busy, decided the moment already passed, this is for you."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [0:52-1:01] ESCALATION TEASE
VO: "And by the end of this I'll give you the one rule that makes sure you never talk yourself out of it again."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [1:01-1:26] WHO THIS IS FOR
VO: "This is for the guy who's completely fine texting, completely fine on a call, and then goes silent walking past someone in real life. It's for the guy who's rehearsed the perfect opener in his head forty times and still hasn't said it out loud. If that's you, you don't have a personality problem, you have an exposure problem, and that's actually good news, because exposure is fixable."
ON-SCREEN: talking head, small text card flashes "EXPOSURE PROBLEM, NOT A PERSONALITY PROBLEM" under the last line then disappears.

## [1:26-1:43] EXCUSE LIST
VO: "Here's the excuse reel. Walking too fast to stop. Headphones in, so you've got a built-in excuse not to hear yourself think. Telling yourself the timing's off, she looks busy, she's probably with someone. And the best one: I'll catch her on the way back. You won't."
FOOTAGE: B-roll from the hesitation clip (a different moment than the cold open) under the first half of the VO, then back to talking head for "You won't."
ON-SCREEN: talking head for the punchline.

## [1:43-1:55] EXCUSE RECAP
VO: "Sound familiar? Too fast to stop. Headphones as a shield. Bad timing, every single time. That's not a coincidence, that's the anxiety talking, and it's got an unlimited supply of reasons."
ON-SCREEN: three quick bullet cards pop in sync with the VO: "TOO FAST TO STOP" / "HEADPHONES AS A SHIELD" / "BAD TIMING, EVERY TIME."

## [1:55-2:10] EPIPHANY
VO: "Here's what nobody tells you. She's not immune to this either. Most people, most of the time, want to be seen, want someone to say hello. You're not the only one standing there working up the nerve, she might be too."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [2:10-2:19] SUBSCRIBE ASK
VO: "If you've ever stood there running that exact excuse reel, subscribe now, because the rest of this is the actual fix, not another pep talk."
ON-SCREEN: subscribe button animation, small and quick, doesn't block the frame.

## [2:19-2:38] PREP DOESN'T WORK
VO: "Quick gut check. How many videos like this one have you already watched about approach anxiety? And be honest, how much has that actually changed what happens when you're standing there? Probably not much, because the fix was never going to come from more information, you've got enough of that already."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [2:38-3:00] WHY IT'S FEAR NOT SKILL
VO: "Here's the proof it was never about ability. If your boss told you that you had to walk up and say hi to get a promotion, you'd do it, immediately, no hesitation. Funny how fast the fear disappears when there's a raise on the line. So the skill was always there. What's missing isn't the ability to approach, it's your permission to feel scared and do it anyway."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [3:00-3:40] THE MECHANISM
VO: "So if it's not a skill problem, what actually shrinks the fear? Not information. Not watching more videos, this one included. Fear shrinks through exposure, the same way it works for literally any other fear. Someone scared of dogs doesn't get less scared by reading about dogs, they get less scared by standing near a calm one a bunch of times until their body learns it's safe. Same thing here. Every single time you approach and the world doesn't end, your nervous system gets a little more proof that this isn't actually dangerous. And when she says no, or she's just not interested, that's not a verdict on you, that's just information. One data point. You collect enough of those and the fear runs out of fuel."
FOOTAGE: 3-4s of street B-roll from the infield source (walking up, no audio from her) under "Every single time you approach and the world doesn't end."
ON-SCREEN: talking head, small text card on "just information" line.

## [3:40-3:54] INFIELD PROOF
VO: "Watch what actually happens when you just go."
FOOTAGE: 1-2s flash back to the hesitation clip (the "before"), then hard cut to the infield clip (not yet selected) showing a good reaction, real mic audio, no added dialogue.
REACTION/OUTCOME: she stops, opens up, body language relaxed, the conversation continues naturally.

## [3:54-4:03] REACTION
VO: "That's it. That's the whole secret. Nothing bad happened. Worst case, a normal, slightly awkward exchange, and you're both fine thirty seconds later."
ON-SCREEN: back to talking head.

## [4:03-4:19] COST OF WAITING
VO: "Here's the trade you're actually making every time you talk yourself out of it. It's not comfort now, embarrassment later. It's comfort now, and a slow drip of regret that just sits there, because you never even find out what would've happened."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [4:19-4:44] MASSIVE ACTION
VO: "Here's where most guys get stuck even after they know all this. They'll do two or three approaches a week and wonder why the fear hasn't moved. At that pace it can take years to build up enough reps to actually desensitize. The fix isn't more thinking between approaches, it's more approaches. Stack them. A rough week for you should look like a good month for most guys."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [4:44-5:10] THE RULE, REMOVE THE CHOICE
VO: "So here's the one rule, straight from the AskToddV approach: remove the choice. Don't decide in the moment whether you're going to approach, decide it in advance and make it non-negotiable. Something like, if I notice her and there's an opening, I go, no debate. You're not asking yourself 'should I' anymore, because you already answered that question this morning. All that's left is doing the thing you already decided."
ON-SCREEN: talking head, text card holds "REMOVE THE CHOICE" for the section.

## [5:10-5:23] INFIELD PROOF 2
VO: "This is that rule in real time, no hesitation window, just straight into it."
FOOTAGE: infield clip (not yet selected) showing a good reaction, real mic audio only.
REACTION/OUTCOME: she engages quickly, easy back-and-forth, no visible resistance from him before making contact.

## [5:23-5:44] WHO YOU BECOME
VO: "Do this for a month and something shifts that has nothing to do with results. You stop replaying the moment you didn't say something. You stop carrying that specific kind of regret home with you. Whether she says yes or no stops being the scariest part of your day, because you already know you can survive either one."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [5:44-5:54] CALLBACK
VO: "That shift is exactly what happened for me. It won't happen from one approach. It happens from doing it enough times that your brain updates the file."
ON-SCREEN: talking head.

## [5:54-6:12] BONUS
VO: "One small bonus if the nerves are still loud right at the moment you walk up: you're already running a scene in your head where it goes badly. You get to pick the movie. Run the version where it goes fine instead, then go find out which one was true."
ON-SCREEN: talking head, on-screen label "BONUS" appears top corner so it reads as extra, not the main point.

## [6:12-6:32] FINAL MONTAGE
VO: "This is what volume actually looks like. Not one perfect approach, a string of them, some flat, some great, all of them proof."
FOOTAGE: quick montage cut from the same infield source clips used above, several beats in a row, no new dialogue.

## [6:32-6:45] CONCRETE ASK
VO: "Here's your assignment. Not someday, this week. Pick a number, five, ten, whatever feels like a stretch, and hit it before Sunday. Not five good ones. Five, period, results don't matter yet, reps do."
ON-SCREEN: talking head, number counter graphic (e.g. "5" or "10") appears briefly.

## [6:45-7:11] CTA / OUTRO
VO: "So don't wait for the fear to go away first, it won't, not from watching, only from doing. Pick one place today, give yourself the rule, no choice, no debate, and go be bad at it on purpose. If you want direct feedback on your specific approach anxiety, I'm running a free Spark Strategy Call, link's in the description and pinned comment. And if this helped, subscribe, there's more of these breakdowns coming."
ON-SCREEN: "SPARK STRATEGY CALL" callout with the on-screen QR code (`video-ideation/spark_strategy_call_qr.png`, calendly.com/thorokafor/spark-strategy) held for the full beat so it's scannable, full link also stated in description and pinned comment, end screen with 2 video recommendations.
