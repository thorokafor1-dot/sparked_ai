# Video Ideation — "Best Places to Cold Approach in Public (TIER LIST)"

## Concept
A tier-list video ranking real-world locations for in-person cold approach (S through F), backed by your own infield footage per location, in the format popularized by Coach Knox's dating tier-list videos — but applied to in-person approach instead of his texting/phone/online-dating topics.

## Title
**Best Places to Cold Approach in Public (TIER LIST)** — locked, matches the Coach Knox title formula (`[Specific Topic] (TIER LIST)`) that's proven for this content type. See prior research thread for source videos.

## Locations Ranked (confirmed: you have real footage from all of these)
Street / walking by, Coffee shop / cafe, Mall / retail, Grocery store, Gym, Bar, Park, Pool, Dance social, College campus, Restaurant.

## Proposed Tier Ranking (draft — needs your correction from real results)
Ranked on: response/opening rate, quality of engagement (relaxed vs. rushed), repeatability (approach volume per session), and difficulty/social friction.

- **S Tier:** Bar/nightlife, Dance social — approach is the expected social context, low guard, built-in rapport (shared activity/proximity for dance social).
- **A Tier:** College campus, Coffee shop — high volume of receptive people, people are seated/stationary or casually social, low rush.
- **B Tier:** Park, Pool — casual and social, but lower density / more situational (weather, time of day).
- **C Tier:** Street / walking by, Mall/retail — high volume but people are moving with intent, more guarded, quicker reads needed.
- **D Tier:** Grocery store, Restaurant — task-focused or already seated/with others, higher friction to open naturally.
- **F Tier:** Gym — she's mid-workout, focused, and this is the location the cold-approach community most consistently flags as intrusive/lowest-success.

**⚠️ Open question — needs your input before the script is finalized:** does this ranking match your actual real-world results? Move anything that's wrong based on your footage/outcomes, not just general consensus.

## Hook Plan

Grounded in `long-form-hook-research/infield/hook_patterns.md` (this channel's In-Person archetypes, priority 1 for this video).

**0:00-0:08 — Zero-setup cold open (Type A, the single highest-scoring archetype in the dataset, 58.6x on `RgDCIvRnfcI`).** Open on a punchy mid-interaction snippet, no branding, no "in this video," pulled from your strongest real reaction across the S-tier footage (bar or dance social). Use the Chris Bizness teaser-then-replay variant: drop the charged line/reaction first, then cut away before it resolves. This clip gets replayed in full later when that location's tier segment plays.

**0:08-0:18 — Stated premise (Type B), delivered to camera.** Cut to you on camera stating the format directly: "I've approached women in every public place you can think of, street, gym, mall, bar, campus. I ranked all 11 S tier to F tier based on what actually happened." Show the tier board graphic (S-F, color-coded per the thumbnail convention already locked) on screen as you say it, this is the visual anchor for the ranking promise.

**0:18-0:26 — Ranking/escalation promise (Type E adaptation, "prove me wrong" stakes-setting).** Tease the most contestable placement without revealing the full order, e.g. naming the F-tier location as a flat statement ("one of these got an F and I already know the comments are going to fight me on it"). This is what makes an 11-item list watchable start to finish, viewers stay to see if their own read matches yours, mirroring how Coach Knox's tier lists generate debate.

**0:26-0:30 — Cut into the countdown, starting F tier and climbing to S.** Starting at the worst tier (not S) builds toward the payoff you just teased at 0:18, and matches the countdown-curiosity mechanic Type B relies on (viewer tracks progress toward the reveal).

**Subscribe ask placement:** Per the cross-cutting finding in `hook_patterns.md` ("no subscribe-ask in the first 90 seconds in any of the top In-Person outliers, unlike the Shorts pacing model"), do not place it in this 0-30s block. Land it after the F-tier segment resolves, around 1:00-1:30, right after the first real payoff/footage clip lands and before moving into E tier.

**Do not include:** channel branding/intro card in the first 30 seconds, a generic "welcome back to the channel" line, or the subscribe ask before the first footage payoff, all confirmed reach-limiters in the pattern research.
