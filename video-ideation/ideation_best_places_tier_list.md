# Video Ideation: "Best Places to Cold Approach in Public (TIER LIST)"

## Concept
A tier-list video ranking real-world locations for in-person cold approach (S through F), backed by your own infield footage per location, in the format popularized by Coach Knox's dating tier-list videos, but applied to in-person approach instead of his texting/phone/online-dating topics.

## Title
**Best Places to Cold Approach in Public (TIER LIST)**, locked, matches the Coach Knox title formula (`[Specific Topic] (TIER LIST)`) that's proven for this content type. See prior research thread for source videos.

## Locations Ranked (confirmed: you have real footage from all of these)
Street / walking by, Coffee shop / cafe, Mall, Grocery store, Gym, Bar, Nightclub, Park, Pool, Beach, Dance social, College campus, Restaurant.

## Proposed Tier Ranking (corrected against your real results)
Ranked on: response/opening rate, quality of engagement (relaxed vs. rushed), repeatability (approach volume per session), and difficulty/social friction. Every location below now has a real pro and con from the creator's own experience, not generic community consensus.

- **S Tier (weakest to strongest): Bar, Nightclub, Dance social.**
  - **Bar:** Pro, people are there to socialize and alcohol lowers resistance to strangers, also a good first venue before moving to a nightclub later. Con, costs money if drinking, drinking isn't healthy long-term, and skipping it can make you feel like the odd one out.
  - **Nightclub:** Pro, same expected-to-approach norm as a bar, plus you can spot pre-existing interest signals before approaching, and the noise forcing physical closeness raises the charge of the interaction. Con, crowd skews short-term/fun-focused, not relationship material.
  - **Dance social:** Pro, shared activity and built-in proximity/rapport before you say a word (beats even bar/nightclub for this), and it starts way earlier too, 7-8pm vs. a nightclub's 11pm. Con, the whole thing hinges on actually having dance skill, doesn't work without it, this conditional ceiling is why it now ranks S tier (moved up from A) rather than a flat, walk-in-and-it-works location.
- **A Tier:**
  - **Pool, Beach:** Pro (shared), less clothing raises baseline energy, nobody's rushed, relaxed/social mood. Con (shared), both seasonal/weather-dependent.
  - **College campus:** Pro, big volume, the crowd skews young so it's exactly your target range, walkable, people feel safe, generally open-minded. Con, rushed if caught between classes, cold approach still isn't the expected norm, and she may ask what you study, awkward if you're not a student.
- **B Tier (weakest to strongest): Park, Gym, Mall.**
  - **Park:** Pro, relaxed, not working, good mood from sunlight/nature. Con, not expecting to be approached (though open to it if read right), situational on weather/time of day.
  - **Gym:** Pro, usually got a pump going (better state) and people naturally take breaks between sets (a real opening window). Con, doing real volume means running into the same women again eventually, caps long-term fresh volume at one location.
  - **Mall:** Pro, people aren't in a hurry, browsing/killing-time energy, plus great instant-date potential (keep walking the mall together, grab food right there). Ranked above both Park and Gym within B tier for this reason. Con, she's still not expecting to be approached here, but she's generally open to the idea, same as most places outside a bar or club.
- **C Tier:**
  - **Coffee shop:** Pro, relaxed, stationary for ~20 minutes. Con, low volume, only a handful of approachable people per visit, can feel random without a reason to be there.
  - **Grocery store:** Pro, it's a safe space and people are somewhat in a social mood. Con, she's on a mission, rarely more than about five minutes before that runs out.
- **D Tier:**
  - **Street:** Pro, basically infinite volume, way less competition from other guys than a bar or nightclub, truly tests your conversation skill from zero. Con, people don't expect a random approach, often headed somewhere, guard up with no shared context to soften it.
- **F Tier:**
  - **Restaurant:** Con, she's usually already in a tight social circle and busy eating, hardest location on the list to open naturally without interrupting both a conversation and a meal.

**Note:** this ranking is now built entirely from real feedback, further correction is still welcome if anything still doesn't match your results.

## Hook Plan

Grounded in `long-form-hook-research/infield/hook_patterns.md` (this channel's In-Person archetypes, priority 1 for this video).

**0:00-0:08: Zero-setup cold open (Type A, the single highest-scoring archetype in the dataset, 58.6x on `RgDCIvRnfcI`).** Open on a punchy mid-interaction snippet, no branding, no "in this video," pulled from your strongest real reaction across the S-tier footage (bar or nightclub, the only two S-tier locations now). Use the Chris Bizness teaser-then-replay variant: drop the charged line/reaction first, then cut away before it resolves. This clip gets replayed in full later when that location's tier segment plays.

**0:08-0:18: Stated premise (Type B), delivered to camera.** Cut to you on camera stating the format directly: "I've approached women in every public place you can think of, street, gym, mall, bar, campus. I ranked all 13 S tier to F tier based on what actually happened." Show the empty tier board (S-F rows, color-coded, tiles unplaced) on screen as you say it, this is the visual anchor for the ranking promise. **Decision: the board is built live on screen during recording (tiles dragged into place in real time as each location is ranked), not a premade finished graphic**, this is a deliberate format choice, viewers watch the ranking actually happen rather than just seeing a finished result.

**0:18-0:26: Ranking/escalation promise (Type E adaptation, "prove me wrong" stakes-setting).** Tease the most contestable placement without revealing the full order, e.g. naming the F-tier location as a flat statement ("one of these got an F and I already know the comments are going to fight me on it"). This is what makes a 13-item list watchable start to finish, viewers stay to see if their own read matches yours, mirroring how Coach Knox's tier lists generate debate.

**0:26-0:30: Cut into the countdown, starting F tier and climbing to S.** Starting at the worst tier (not S) builds toward the payoff you just teased at 0:18, and matches the countdown-curiosity mechanic Type B relies on (viewer tracks progress toward the reveal).

**Subscribe ask placement:** Per `hook_patterns.md`'s finding that top In-Person outliers don't front-load the subscribe ask into the opening hook block (unlike the Shorts pacing model), don't place it in this 0-30s block. Land it right after the F-tier segment resolves, around 1:00-1:20, once the first real payoff/footage clip has landed.

**Do not include:** channel branding/intro card in the first 30 seconds, a generic "welcome back to the channel" line, or the subscribe ask before the first footage payoff, all confirmed reach-limiters in the pattern research.
