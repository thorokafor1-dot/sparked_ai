## The gap (evidence)
1. **Their titles never use the words men search.** Across the 152 recent titles from Jameer, Jay Throck, Zaen Azzuri, ItsMP3, LilPraisey, Edtki and Teige, "flirt" appears **0** times and "attractive" **0** times. "Same line", "chase", "blush" and "tier list" also never appear (true of this 152-title sample, not proof that no channel anywhere uses them), and only 3 titles rate anything. Every title is entertainment framing ("rizzing baddies").
2. **The audience is searching for answers.** YouTube autocomplete (US, 2026-10-09) shows what people type, not how many people type it, and these channels may win more through browse than search. It suggests:
   - "what women find attractive in men" (+ comparison / physically / interview / tier list)
   - "women rate men's outfits / cologne / beards / facial hair / hairstyles"
   - "pick up lines that actually work / make a girl blush"
   - "how to flirt without being obvious"
   - "how to flirt on facetime"
   - "how to get a girl to chase you"
   - "what girls think vs what boys think"
3. **Other niches prove those questions get clicks:**
   - Zoomology "The Science Behind What Women Find Attractive", 433K
   - Psych2Go "Flirting vs. Being Friendly: How to Tell the Difference", 391K
   - ThinkDot "5 Signs You're Actually Very Attractive", 277K (score 43)
   - Elvzen "15 Clear Signs You're a Highly Attractive Man", 156K (score 57)
   - Dylan McKnight "I tried to 'Cold Approach' 100 girls without any experience (Introvert vs Girls)", 2.4M (score 80)
   - Sprouht "I Asked 100 Married Couples for Dating Advice", 974K (score 33)
   - Coach Knox "Signs She Likes You (TIER LIST)"

   But these channels are talking heads or interviews with **no real flirting footage as proof.** The count and interview hits (Dylan McKnight, Sprouht) back the count mechanic, while the talking-head hits back the topic. Neither proves the topic transfers to e-date footage. That transfer is what the first episodes test.
4. **The video-chat mechanics are already proven:** the variable test (Teige 5.0x, ItsMP3 5.4x), counts and checklists (bingo 108x), and many women in one sitting.

**Sparked's edge:** answer the question men search, and prove it live on real e-dates. Their footage plus the psychology channels' question equals a title neither side can make. It's also the brand promise (learn to spark attraction), so every video teaches.

**Shoot first:** #1, #2 and #3. They're controlled and countable, so the result is real either way. #6 moves up only if the footage audit finds 5+ distinct signs. Test #1 before #5, because ItsMP3's pickup-line video got only 0.44x, and framing it as women's verdicts is a hypothesis, not proof.

**Weak spot:** search-style titles are flatter than "rizzing baddies" browse bait (no person, no tension in the words). The thumbnail and the first 5 seconds carry the emotion: her real reaction, and the scoreboard or tally as the text.

The first episode of each series is the test: whichever beats your channel's median becomes the "Pt. 2". All formats pair with the thumbnail system in `thumbnail-creation/videochat_thumbnail_strategy.md` (scoreboard or tally = mechanic text; signs = labelled reveal).

**Honest caveat:** these are new combinations of proven parts (proven demand plus a proven video-chat mechanic), not exact copies of a proven video. That's the price of beating the genre instead of matching it. One near-miss: ItsMP3's "I Tested VIRAL PICKUP LINES" got only 0.44x. That's why #2 and #4 are framed as women ranking, or one controlled line, not as "viral lines".
